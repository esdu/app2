# Opt-in post-roll (#10)

The persisted **Keep after Save** preference offers Off (default), 5 seconds and 10 seconds. Old preference records decode to Off. Choices are disabled throughout startup, capture, collection, export and lease release. The selected buffer duration remains unchanged. A longer post-roll option requires a separate storage evaluation.

The capture queue registers an immutable interval at Save: `[accepted video end - buffer duration, accepted video end + post-roll]`. It immediately pins completed pre-trigger segments and adds every overlapping finalized future segment to the same lease **before pruning**. Export uses that original start even when the end is shortened. Waiting and taking a later retrospective snapshot would lose the beginning and is deliberately avoided.

Positive post-roll keeps ordinary intake running and shows accessible remaining media time. The save remains active until protected staging/local-library commit and source lease release. Off retains the original immediate, briefly freezing snapshot implementation. The internal `snapshotKeepingIntake(postRoll:onProgress:)` seam explicitly supports zero, 5 and 10 seconds for the #11 comparison; calling it with zero closes the current segment while continuing intake, without changing the production Off route. DEBUG diagnostics expose received/accepted, save-dropped and backpressured video/audio counts, outstanding writers and leases.

## Stop and failure behavior

- Stop Buffer, library entry, background and camera/audio interruption stop capture immediately. They finish only media already accepted, retain the original requested start, and return a shorter snapshot when safe finalized video exists. The saved result says “Shorter moment saved. Recording stopped before post-roll finished.” Nothing restarts automatically, including on foreground return.
- A video stall uses the independent five-second host watchdog and the same shorter-save path. Collection also has a host deadline of post-roll plus five seconds, so a stalled media clock cannot wait forever.
- Once the cutoff or stop is reached, relevant finalizers have at most five host seconds. A timeout cancels the collection, releases its lease and cancels encoding work. Capture stays stopped, and terminal errors propagate through the engine stop/failure callback so hardware capture and UI stop too. Later callbacks cannot mutate a newer capture generation.
- Low disk/aggregate file guard stops intake and tries the same safe shorter-save path. Writer failure, unavailable media, cancellation or inability to preserve the pinned interval produces a visible error, never a complete-duration success. Task cancellation releases the collection lease; cancellation after stop also cancels outstanding writers and rejects their late callbacks. Explicit restart cancels an old pending request.
- One save is allowed through collection, export and release. The ordinary limit of three finalizing writers, completed-media quota of 160 MiB, aggregate file guard of 192 MiB, 64 MiB disk reserve, protected files and staged persistent library flow remain in force. Stop/restart actively cancels outstanding finalizers rather than hiding them by resetting a counter.

Only finalizers intersecting the immutable interval block collection completion; later ordinary intake cannot keep a completed request waiting. Leases can keep boundary files beyond normal time retention, but do not waive storage limits. If the requested pinned history cannot fit, saving fails explicitly instead of evicting its beginning.

Independent microphone callbacks are handled at segment boundaries: overlapping PCM packets are clipped to the new segment start and late tails reach bounded closing writers before finalization. Lookahead is bounded to 64 packets / 2 MiB, and at most two closing writers may wait at an immediate-save rollover, each with a one-second host deadline and counted against the existing writer budget. `AudioBoundaryTests` exercises independent 10 ms packet cadence, video-leading/audio-leading delivery and decoded rollover audio. Physical microphone timing remains an acceptance gate.
